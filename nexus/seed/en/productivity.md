# Productivity and Learning
categoria: Productivity

Practical methods to organize your time, stay focused and learn faster.

## Pomodoro Technique
1. Pick a task.
2. Work for 25 minutes with full focus (one "pomodoro"), notifications off.
3. Take a 5-minute break.
4. After 4 pomodoros, take a longer 15 to 30-minute break.
Variations: 50/10 for deep work. Ask NEXUS to "start a pomodoro" and it will set a reminder for the end of the cycle.

## Eisenhower Matrix
Sort tasks by urgency and importance:
- Urgent and important → do it now (crises, same-day deadlines).
- Important, not urgent → schedule it (planning, learning, health, relationships). This is where most value lies.
- Urgent, not important → delegate or minimize (many interruptions and emails).
- Neither → eliminate.

## GTD (Getting Things Done)
David Allen's method in 5 steps:
1. Capture: write down everything on your mind in a trusted place (NEXUS can be that inbox).
2. Clarify: is it actionable? What is the very next physical action?
3. Organize: next actions, projects, waiting for, someday/maybe, calendar.
4. Reflect: weekly review of all lists.
5. Engage: choose what to do based on context, time and energy.
Two-minute rule: if it takes less than 2 minutes, do it now.

## Time blocking and deep work
- Time blocking: reserve calendar blocks for each kind of work, like meetings with yourself.
- Deep work (Cal Newport): long distraction-free periods for cognitively demanding tasks. Schedule them at your peak-energy time.
- Batch shallow tasks (email, messages) into fixed windows.
- Silence notifications and leave your phone in another room while focusing.

## SMART goals and OKRs
SMART goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
Weak: "I want to read more." Strong: "Read 12 books by December, 20 pages a day."
OKRs: one inspiring Objective with 2 to 4 measurable Key Results, reviewed quarterly.

## Habits
- Habit loop: cue → routine → reward.
- Start ridiculously small (1 push-up, 1 page) and grow later.
- Habit stacking: "After [current habit], I will [new habit]."
- Make good habits obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying; bad ones invisible, unattractive, difficult and unsatisfying (ideas popularized by James Clear).
- Don't break the chain: track completed days. If you miss once, never miss twice in a row.

## How to study and learn
- Active recall: test yourself instead of re-reading. Close the material and try to remember.
- Spaced repetition: review at growing intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month). Tools like Anki automate this.
- Feynman technique: explain the topic in simple words, as if to a child; where you get stuck, go back and study.
- Interleaving: mix problem types instead of drilling one at a time.
- Sleep well: sleep consolidates memory.
- Teach what you learn: one of the most effective ways to retain it.

## Weekly review
Once a week, for 30 minutes:
1. Empty your inboxes (notes, email, messages).
2. Review last week's and next week's calendar.
3. Update your task and project lists.
4. Choose the week's top 3 priorities.
5. Reflect: what worked, what didn't, what to change.
