# First Aid Manual
categoria: First Aid

Quick reference guide. It does not replace hands-on training or professional care.
In any serious emergency call first: 911 (USA/Canada), 112 (EU and most of the world), 999 (UK), 192/193 (Brazil).
Poisoning (USA): Poison Control 1-800-222-1222.

## Golden rules: safety and calling for help
1. Make sure the scene is safe for you (traffic, fire, electricity, gas). Don't become a second victim.
2. Check the person: tap their shoulders and shout. Do they respond? Are they breathing normally?
3. Call emergency services and put the phone on speaker. Give the address, what happened, number of victims and their condition.
4. Send someone to fetch an AED (automated external defibrillator) — malls, airports and gyms often have one.
5. Don't give food or drink to unconscious or seriously injured people.
6. Don't move trauma victims (falls, crashes) unless there is immediate danger — there may be a spinal injury.

## Cardiac arrest (CPR)
Signs: the person doesn't respond and isn't breathing, or is only gasping.
1. Call emergency services and ask for an AED.
2. Lay the person on their back on a firm surface.
3. Place interlocked hands in the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), arms straight.
4. Push hard and fast: 100 to 120 compressions per minute, 5 to 6 cm (2 to 2.4 in) deep in adults, letting the chest fully recoil.
5. If untrained, do continuous hands-only CPR. If trained: 30 compressions to 2 rescue breaths.
6. As soon as the AED arrives, turn it on and follow the voice prompts. It only shocks if needed.
7. Don't stop until the person responds, professionals take over, or you're exhausted. Swap rescuers every 2 minutes.
Children: about 5 cm (2 in, one third of chest depth). Infants: 2 fingers in the center of the chest, about 4 cm (1.5 in).

## Choking
If the person is coughing forcefully and can speak: encourage coughing, don't slap their back.
If they can't speak, breathe or cough (hands at throat):
1. Adults and children over 1 year: 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades, person leaning forward.
2. Then 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver): from behind, fist just above the navel, pull inward and upward.
3. Alternate 5 blows and 5 thrusts until the object comes out.
4. Pregnant or obese people: chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts.
5. If they become unresponsive: call for help and start CPR; when opening the mouth, remove the object only if you can see it.
Infants (under 1): face down along your forearm, head lower, 5 back blows; turn over and give 5 chest thrusts with 2 fingers. Never do abdominal thrusts on infants.

## Bleeding
1. Protect your hands (gloves or a plastic bag) if possible.
2. Apply firm, direct pressure on the wound with a clean cloth or gauze and keep pressing.
3. If it soaks through, don't remove it: add another layer on top and keep pressing.
4. Life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg that pressure doesn't stop: apply a tourniquet 5 to 7 cm (2 to 3 in) above the wound, tighten until bleeding stops and note the time. Don't loosen it.
5. Embedded objects: don't pull them out; stabilize around them and get help.
6. Nosebleed: lean slightly FORWARD and pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 to 15 minutes. Don't tilt the head back.

## Burns
1. Remove the person from the heat source. If clothing catches fire: stop, drop and roll.
2. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes.
3. Remove rings, watches and nearby clothing before swelling — but not anything stuck to the skin.
4. Cover loosely with cling film or a clean, damp cloth.
5. DON'T use ice, butter, toothpaste or home remedies. Don't pop blisters.
6. Seek care if the burn is larger than the person's palm, affects the face, hands, feet, genitals or joints, is chemical or electrical, or the person is a child or elderly.
Chemical burns: flush with plenty of running water for 20 minutes or more. Electrical: switch off the power before touching the person.

## Fractures, sprains and falls
1. Don't try to straighten the bone.
2. Immobilize it in the position found, including the joints above and below (splints from cardboard, magazines, pillows).
3. Open fracture: cover the wound with a clean cloth without pushing the bone back.
4. Sprain: rest, ice wrapped in cloth for 15 to 20 minutes, light compression and elevation.
5. Suspected spinal injury (fall from height, crash, diving into shallow water): don't move the person, keep head and neck aligned and call emergency services.

## Seizures
1. Clear the area and cushion the head.
2. Don't restrain the person and NEVER put anything in their mouth (they can't "swallow their tongue").
3. Time the seizure.
4. When the jerking stops, put them in the recovery position.
5. Call emergency services if it lasts over 5 minutes, another seizure follows, it's their first seizure, they're injured or pregnant, or they don't regain consciousness.

## Stroke — BE FAST
Acting fast saves brain tissue:
- B — Balance: sudden loss of balance or dizziness?
- E — Eyes: sudden vision loss?
- F — Face: ask them to smile. Does one side droop?
- A — Arms: ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down?
- S — Speech: is speech slurred or strange?
- T — Time: any of these signs → call emergency services immediately and note when symptoms started.

## Heart attack
Signs: chest pain, pressure or tightness lasting more than a few minutes, possibly spreading to the left arm, jaw, back or stomach; shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea. Women, the elderly and diabetics may have atypical symptoms (fatigue, discomfort, back pain).
1. Call emergency services immediately.
2. Keep the person seated and at rest; loosen tight clothing.
3. Chewing aspirin only if the dispatcher advises and the person isn't allergic.
4. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing: start CPR.

## Fainting
1. Lay the person on their back and raise their legs about 30 cm (12 in).
2. Loosen clothing and ensure fresh air.
3. If they don't regain consciousness within 1 minute, call for help. If not breathing, start CPR.
4. When they wake up, don't let them stand suddenly; offer water only when fully alert.

## Recovery position
For an unresponsive person who is breathing normally, with no suspected spinal injury:
1. Kneel beside them. Place the arm nearest you at a right angle.
2. Bring the far arm across the chest, back of the hand against their cheek.
3. Bend the far knee and roll them toward you by pulling on the knee.
4. Tilt the head back slightly to keep the airway open. Keep checking breathing.

## Poisoning
1. Call Poison Control (USA 1-800-222-1222) or emergency services. Have the product container at hand.
2. Don't induce vomiting and don't give milk or home remedies.
3. Product on skin or in eyes: rinse with running water for 15 to 20 minutes.
4. Gas or smoke: move the person to fresh air if it's safe for you.

## Low blood sugar, heatstroke and electric shock
Hypoglycemia (diabetic who is shaky, sweaty, confused): if conscious and able to swallow, give 15 g of fast sugar (half a glass of juice or regular soda, 1 tablespoon of sugar). If unconscious, nothing by mouth: call emergency services.
Heatstroke (hot skin, confusion, high temperature): move to shade, cool with water and wet cloths, give water if conscious, and call for help if confused or fainting.
Electric shock: switch off the power before touching the person; use a dry non-conductive object (wood, plastic) to move the wire. Check breathing and start CPR if needed.
