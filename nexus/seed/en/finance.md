# Personal Finance
categoria: Finance

Basic financial literacy concepts. Educational information, not individual investment advice. Product names and tax rules vary by country.

## Budgeting: where the money goes
1. Write down your monthly net income (what actually reaches your account).
2. Track every expense for 30 days, including small ones (coffee, apps, delivery).
3. Group them: fixed (rent, bills), variable (groceries, transport) and discretionary.
4. The 50/30/20 rule as a starting point: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% financial goals (emergency fund, debt, investing).
5. "Pay yourself first": move the savings portion as soon as you get paid, not whatever is left over.
Tip: ask NEXUS to log expenses ("I spent $12 on lunch") and total them by category later.

## Emergency fund
Money for the unexpected (job loss, health, repairs), not for opportunities.
- Size: 3 to 6 months of living expenses; 6 to 12 months if self-employed or with irregular income.
- Where: safe, liquid places such as a high-yield savings account, money market fund or short-term government bonds (e.g. US Treasury bills).
- Priority: build it before taking investment risk.

## Debt
- Credit card balances usually carry the highest interest rates: pay them off first.
- Avalanche method: pay the highest-interest debt first (saves the most money).
- Snowball method: pay the smallest balance first (builds motivation with quick wins).
- Negotiate and refinance expensive debt into cheaper debt when possible.
- Paying only the minimum makes debt grow quickly.

## Compound interest
Interest on interest: each period's return also starts earning.
Formula: A = P × (1 + r)^n — amount = principal × (1 + rate) to the power of the number of periods.
Example: $10,000 at 7% a year for 30 years ≈ $76,000.
Rule of 72: divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes to double (at 8% ≈ 9 years).
It also works against you: compounding debt grows exponentially.
The most powerful factor is time: starting early, even with little, makes a big difference.

## Inflation
Inflation is the general rise in prices, which reduces purchasing power. Cash sitting idle loses value. Real return = return minus inflation. An investment returning 10% a year with 4% inflation has a real gain of about 5.8% (1.10 / 1.04 − 1).

## Fixed income
You lend money and receive interest under defined terms.
- Government bonds (e.g. US Treasuries; I Bonds and TIPS protect against inflation).
- Certificates of deposit (CDs) and high-yield savings accounts.
- Corporate bonds: higher yield, higher credit risk.
- Deposit insurance protects bank deposits up to a limit (USA: FDIC, $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category; EU: €100,000).
- Bond prices fall when interest rates rise; longer maturities are more sensitive.

## Stocks and funds
- Stocks: small pieces of companies. Higher potential return with volatility; think 5+ years.
- Index funds and ETFs: track a market index (e.g. S&P 500, total world market). Cheap, simple diversification; low fees matter a lot over decades.
- REITs: real estate companies that pay out most of their income as dividends.
- Never invest in what you don't understand. Be suspicious of high, guaranteed, fast returns — the hallmark of scams and Ponzi schemes.

## Diversification and risk profile
"Don't put all your eggs in one basket": spreading across asset classes, sectors and countries reduces risk.
Profiles: conservative (safety first), moderate (accepts some volatility) and aggressive (accepts large swings for higher returns).
Allocation depends on goal and time horizon: short term (under 2 years) → liquid fixed income; long term → can include stocks.
Rebalance periodically to keep your chosen proportions.

## Goals and retirement
Set SMART goals: "save $6,000 for a trip in 18 months" → about $333 a month plus interest.
Retirement accounts (USA): employer 401(k) — always capture the full employer match, it's free money — and IRAs (Traditional: tax deduction now; Roth: tax-free withdrawals later).
4% rule (a common rule of thumb): to live off investments, save about 25 times your annual expenses.

## Habits and scams
- Impulse purchases: wait 24 to 48 hours before unplanned buys.
- "Buy now, pay later" still commits future income; add up all installments.
- Banks never ask for your password, one-time codes, or to move money to a "safe account".
- Be wary of urgency, gift-card payment requests, and "relatives" texting from new numbers.
- Review subscriptions monthly and cancel the ones you don't use.
